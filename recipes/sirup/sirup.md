# Floral or herbal sirup

I'll write a recipe for elderflower sirup, but the process is same for all floral sirups. The ratio of sugar to water can range from 1:1 to 2:1. More sugar offers more time before it goes bad, but sacrifices taste. <br/>
All these amounts are just very rough estimates, feel free to adjust them as you wish. Mix different herbs, use limes instead of lemons, do what you want. 

## Ingredients
* 1.5 kg sugar
* 1 l water
* 15-25 elderflower blossoms, remove stems and leaves
* 20 g citric acid
* 2 lemons

## Instructions
Put the blossoms and lemon slices into a clean, heat-proof jar (at least 2.5 l, it should fit everything). Put the lemon slices on top, they'll keep the blooms submerged.<br/>
Put sugar, water, and citric acid into a pot, bring to a boil. You can let it cool a bit - it depends on the jar and what herbs you're using. Elderflower has some delicate volatile compounds that break at higher temperatures, so if you let the liquid cool, you'll preserve more of them. <br/>
Pour the sugar mixture over the flowers. Cover with an airtight lid and let it cool. Steep for about two days. You can stir it once or twice a day. <br/>
Rinse bottles with boiling water to clean them. Strain the sirup into the bottles through a fine sieve or a cheesecloth. Store in fridge, or go through a proper canning procedure to make it shelf-stable.<br/>
Notes: People's opinions differ on whether to wash the flowers or not before use. I like to wash mine to remove bugs and debris, but it removes some flavour. I add extra blooms to compensate for it. Definitely wash the lemon though. <br/>

# Fruit sirup

There's a thing called cordial too? I guess it has spices and herbs and nuts included for extra flavour profile. The ratio of sugar to water can range from 1:1 to 2:1. More sugar offers more time before it goes bad, but sacrifices taste. <br/>
All these amounts are just very rough estimates, feel free to adjust them as you wish. <br/>
Fruit sirup goes bad faster than a floral one. 

## Ingredients

* 1 kg sugar 1
* 0.5 l water
* 1 kg strawberries
* 20 g citric acid

## Instructions
Clean and chop the strawberries. <br/>
Place sugar, water and citric acid in a pan, heat until dissolved, then add the strawberries. If you can't be arsed, just toss it all in and cook gently until the fruit breaks down. <br/>
Strain into a bottle using a fine sieve or a cheesecloth. Store in a fridge or follow a proper canning procedure to make it shelf-stable.<br/>
