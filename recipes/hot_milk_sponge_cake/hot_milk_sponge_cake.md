A lovely cake; chewy (unless I fucked it up?), moist, velvety, somewhat like a pudding

## Ingredients
* 2 Large Eggs room temperature
* 200 g Granulated Sugar (caster sugar)
* 120 ml Milk
* 57 g Butter
* 113 g Cake Flour
* 1 teaspoon Baking Powder
* ½ teaspoon Salt
* 1 vanilla bean

## Instructions
Preheat the oven to 170°C (150° fan-assisted) and grease a 20cm cake pan.<br/>
In a bowl, mix flour, baking powder, and salt. Set aside. <br/>
In a stand mixer with the whisk attachment, whip the eggs and sugar together on high speed for 5 full minutes until they are thick, pale, and doubled in volume. If you drizzle the batter on top of itself, it should leave a little trail.<br/>
Meanwhile, warm the butter, milk, and vanilla bean in a saucepan over medium heat. Scrape the vanilla in, then throw the pod in there too. It should be just hot enough that the butter melts - do not let it boil.<br/>
Sift of the flour mixture to the egg/sugar mixture and fold it in gently. You can either do this by hand with a rubber spatula or continue to use the whisk attachment on the stand mixer with the lowest speed setting. <br/>
Then sift in the other half of the flour mixture and repeat. Be very careful not to knock too much air out of the batter. <br/>
Gradually pour the hot milk mixture into the cake batter. If you are folding by hand, add a little milk at a time and fold gently. If you are using a stand mixer, keep it on the lowest speed setting while you slowly drizzle the milk in. Again, be very careful not to overmix or knock too much air out of the batter.<br/>
Scrape down the bottom of the bowl to make sure everything is incorporated. The batter is a bit thin and if you fail this step, there might be liquid that flows out of your cake tin. <br/>
Pour the sponge cake batter into the prepared cake pan and bake for 30-35 minutes or until a skewer inserted in the middle comes out clean. I recommend placing the tins on a tray, just in case they flow out. <br/>
Let cool in the pan for 10 minutes, gently run a spatula around the edge, then invert onto a wire rack to cool completely. <br/>
